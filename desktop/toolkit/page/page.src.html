Pattern Toolwall

A wall of practical tools for understanding and changing the patterns you repeat. Each one is a short worksheet you can fill in and save, with a plain explanation and the three best tools to try next.

What this is, and how to use itClick to hide or show

This is a toolbox for changing patterns: things you keep doing that you would rather not, such as scrolling late at night, snacking when stressed, putting things off, or reacting the same way in every argument. It gathers the practical tools from the best-studied approaches to behaviour change and therapy, and turns each one into a short worksheet you can fill in, save and come back to.

You do not need any background to use it. Every tool opens with a plain explanation of what it is, why it helps and when to use it, and every worksheet question is written as a full sentence. Nothing here replaces a therapist, and a few tools say clearly that they are meant to be done with one.

How to use it

  1. Start with the Pattern log in the Habit section. Fill it in each time the pattern happens, for about two weeks. Most patterns turn out to have two or three clear triggers.
  2. Open the Start here section and run the Capability, opportunity, motivation check. It tells you what is missing, and links the tools that fit.
  3. Pick one or two tools, not ten. Open a tool, read its short introduction, answer the questions and press Save entry. Mark it Trying so it appears in My kit with a start date.
  4. Open the tool's Next steps tab to see the three best tools to use after it, and why. The Path view shows every tool laid out from understanding a pattern to keeping the change.
  5. Each time you save an entry, the tool writes a short summary of it. Press Add to my board to keep the ones that matter on My board, sorted into what you have learned, your plans, what you are using and your wins.
  6. Once a week, use the Weekly review or the Two-phase self-test to check whether the tool is working, then keep it, drop it or swap it.

A few words you will see

Pattern
A behaviour, thought or reaction that keeps repeating.
Cue or trigger
Whatever sets the pattern off: a time, place, person, feeling or the thing you did just before.
Payoff
What the pattern gives you in the short term, such as relief, comfort or escape from boredom.
Capability, opportunity, motivation
The three things any behaviour needs: being able to do it, having surroundings that allow it, and wanting it.
Schema
A deep, long-held belief about yourself or other people, usually formed in childhood.
Technique
One of 93 standard ingredients researchers use to describe exactly what a behaviour change method does. They are listed at the bottom of the wall, each with a plan you can fill in.
Stage
Where a tool fits on the path: understand, decide, plan, act, in the moment, or review and keep.
