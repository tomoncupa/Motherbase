# SPEAK — the research behind the numbers

Gathered 2026-09-15 for `speak/`. Every target, threshold and WHY line in the
app traces to a line here, and every line here has its source beside it.

**How it was gathered, honestly.** The session's network blocked direct page
fetches for every domain except GitHub, so each figure below comes from a
search-engine extract of the cited page rather than the full paper. A vendor
claim, a self-report, or a figure with no primary source is labelled as such.
Where nothing was found, it says so. Two widely quoted "studies" were not
found at all and are not in the app: "National Center for Voice and Speech
says 150 to 160 WPM is optimal" and "a University of Michigan study found 150
to 160 WPM gave 22% higher comprehension"; both appear only on vendor blogs.

FLOW's "Hook, point, close" is 30 seconds with no gap over 1.5s; "Story in

**Syllables.** English articulation rate 6.19 syllables a second in read speech
with pauses excluded (Pellegrino, Coupé & Marsico 2011, [Language Log](https://languagelog.ldc.upenn.edu/nll/?p=22)); about 9 a second is the upper bound of hearing ([NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10531499)); Tauroza & Allison call under 3.17 slow and over 5.33 fast; the cluttering literature puts average conversation at 4 to 5 and says over 5.3 may indicate cluttering ([toofastforwords](https://toofastforwords.com/symptoms-cluttering/main/speech-rate/)). **Syllables per word in adult conversational English: 1.4 to 1.5** ([Journal of Fluency Disorders](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0094730X00000887)); written text 1.45 to 1.65 ([Teflpedia](https://teflpedia.com/Syllables_per_word)).

**What the app took.** Pace with no transcript is syllables divided by 1.4,
and the screen says "estimated from N syllables". 150 words a minute times 1.4
is 3.5 syllables a second including pauses; the beat drill clicks at the middle
of the band times 1.4.

## 3. Filler words

- Bortfeld et al 2001, task conversation: 5.97 disfluencies per 100 words; ums and uhs 3.04 (men) and 2.07 (women) per 100; repeats 1.74 and 1.21. Task role and topic difficulty mattered more than age ([SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/00238309010440020101), [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11575901/)). The usual summary is 2 to 6 per 100 words ([Lund thesis](https://lup.lub.lu.se/student-papers/record/9078253/file/9078254.pdf)).
- Fox Tree 1995 and Shriberg 1994, Switchboard: about 6 per 100 words disfluent, fillers a third to over half of them ([PMC13494130](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13494130/), [Shriberg](https://www.sri.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/disfluencies_in_switchboard.pdf)).
- Clark & Fox Tree 2002, London-Lund: uh plus um at 1.7 per 100 words, individual speakers from 0.12 to 8.85 per 100 ([MDPI Languages](https://doi.org/10.3390/languages8020100)). Uh marks a minor delay, um a major one.
- Per minute: 3.82 filled pauses a minute, range 0.8 to 9.5 across speakers, in Hungarian spontaneous speech ([MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2226-471X/8/1/79)). Toastmasters' arithmetic: 6 per 100 words at 150 WPM is 9 a minute for an untrained speaker ([Toastmasters Magazine](https://www.toastmasters.org/magazine/magazine-issues/2017/september-2017/umah)).
- Filler vs discourse marker: um and uh are filled pauses; "you know", "I mean", "like", "actually", "basically", "so", "okay", "right" are discourse markers with functions, and the same word can be either ([Pacific Standard on Fox Tree](https://psmag.com/social-justice/um-like-know-language-words-valley-girl-81572/), [arXiv 2509.20237](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.20237)). Toastmasters' Ah-Counter counts sounds, crutch words ("and, well, but, so, you know") and repeated words, and reports what was overused; it sets no number ([Toastmasters](https://www.toastmasters.org/membership/club-meeting-roles/ah-counter)).
- What counts as good: Yoodli's target is under 3% of words ([Yoodli](https://yoodli.ai/blog/yoodli-skill-reduce-um-filler-words)). At 150 WPM that is 4.5 a minute; "one or two a minute" is about 1%. Articulate claims elite speakers use fillers five times less than average, method unstated ([Articulate](https://tryarticulate.app/blog/what-ai-speech-analysis-reveals-about-habits-worlds-top-speakers)).
- Silence instead of a filler: listeners understand as fast after a filler as after a silent pause of the same length (Brennan & Schober 2001, [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0749596X00927530)), but fillers lower what listeners think of the speaker's certainty and knowledge, and silent pauses do not have the same effect ([MPI](https://pure.mpg.de/rest/items/item_442640/component/file_442639/content)). Filled pauses can help recall of the next words (Fraundorf & Watson 2011, [PMC3134332](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3134332/)), so neither is free; the pause costs less socially. Speaking faster raises the filler rate ([Yoodli](https://yoodli.ai/blog/how-to-stop-using-filler-words)).

**What the app took.** CLEAN step 1 is a count with nothing to meet; step 2
is 4 a minute (the 3% line); step 3 is 2 a minute; step 4 is zero crutch
phrases; step 5 is fillers at 1 a minute with three pauses or more. Um, uh, er,
erm, ah, hmm, mm always count. `right`, `like`, `so`, `okay`, `actually` count
only at the edge of a transcript chunk or beside a joining word, which is where
they are a crutch and not a word. The default crutch list is Toastmasters'
crutch words plus the two Tom named.

## 4. Pauses

- The standard research threshold is 250ms (de Jong & Bosker 2013, [MPI](https://www.mpi.nl/publications/item1900232/choosing-threshold-silent-pauses-measure-second-language-fluency)); thresholds in the literature run from 100ms to 1s, and 200 to 250ms is where listeners notice a pause ([Zellner](https://web-archive.southampton.ac.uk/cogprints.org/884/3/Zellner.SpeechPauses.pdf)). 90% of the silences inside articulation (stop closures) are under 100ms ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/334061402_Evaluating_Measures_of_Pausing_for_Second_Language_Fluency_Research)).
- Long pauses: 0.5 to 1.5s short, 1.5s and over long, within a turn ([arXiv 2106.15684](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.15684)); 0.6s within a sentence sounds most natural, 0.6 or 1.2s between sentences ([Frontiers in Psychology 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.778018/full)); dramatic pauses in storytelling run 1 to 2s ([UH Law](https://www.law.uh.edu/blakely/story-telling/4%20-%20PAUSES.pdf)).
- Where a pause sits matters more than how many: mid-clause pauses are what listeners hear as disfluent ([Cambridge SSLA](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/studies-in-second-language-acquisition/article/effects-of-speaking-task-and-proficiency-on-the-midclause-pausing-characteristics-of-l1-and-l2-speech-from-the-same-speakers/FFE4407BEAF2A0E55D4D797C9AFE80DD)). No per-minute pause rate for fluent native speakers was found. Articulate's vendor claim: top speakers pause up to five times a minute.
- **De Jong & Wempe 2009**, syllable nuclei from intensity ([Behavior Research Methods](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/BRM.41.2.385), [PDF](https://www.fon.hum.uva.nl/archive/2009/2009-brm-JongWempe.pdf)): an intensity contour in dB; a silence floor about 25dB under the maximum; local peaks kept only with a dip of at least 2dB before them; unvoiced peaks discarded with a pitch track; silences over 250ms counted as pauses; outputs syllables, pauses, phonation time, speech rate (syllables over total time) and articulation rate (syllables over phonation time). Validated by "high correlations" with hand counts on two Dutch corpora; the r values were behind the blocked fetch and are not quoted.

**What the app took, and what it changed.** The ear uses 10ms frames, a
speech floor at the larger of 10dB over the quietest tenth and 25dB under the
loudest, 250ms for a pause and 1.5s for a long one, and a pitch track for
voicing. The peak dip was tuned on four clips with counted syllables on
2026-09-15 (a JFK clip of 29 syllables, a TIMIT sentence of 14, "one two
three", and a synthetic clip of 51): a 2dB dip with 50ms smoothing read 22, 11,
3 and 51; a 1dB dip with 30ms smoothing read 29, 13, 3 and 51. The 1dB dip
ships. That is four clips and 97 syllables, so it is a tuning, not a
validation.

## 5. Mumbling and stuttering

- Clear speech has longer vowels, more intensity, an expanded vowel space and a more variable pitch ([PMC4048446](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4048446/), [PMC3947593](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3947593/), [PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0160088)). Louder speech flattens the spectral tilt, putting more energy in the high frequencies, and that flattening raises intelligibility ([Lu & Cooke](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/222428561_The_contribution_of_changes_in_F0_and_spectral_tilt_to_increased_intelligibility_of_speech_produced_in_noise)). Mumbling can be simulated by reducing jaw movement and neutralising the vowel space ([arXiv 1903.12316](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.12316)).
- Consonants need 2 to 4kHz; the 2kHz octave carries about 30% of intelligibility ([JBL technical note](http://proaudioencyclopedia.com/speech-intelligibility-a-jbl-professional-technical-note-technical-notes-volume-1-no-26/), [DPA](https://www.dpamicrophones.com/mic-university/background-knowledge/facts-about-speech-intelligibility/)). No study validates a single mumble score.
- Drills clinics use: over-articulated reading at half pace ([Besser Sprechen](https://bessersprechen.app/en/stop-mumbling)); a pencil or cork between the teeth ([Vulpine Creations](https://vulpinecreations.com/blog/520-the-pencil-exercise-a-radio-producers-trick-for-troublesome-words)); tongue twisters and reading aloud 10 to 20 minutes a day, with clinic claims of change in weeks ([Speak With Stephanie](https://www.speakwithstephanie.com/post/speaking-clearer-a-guide-to-reduce-mumbling-in-adults)). Practitioner lore; no controlled trial found for any of them.
- Speaking to a metronome reduces the variability of speech movements in typical speakers and in people who stutter ([PMC11482672](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11482672/)); in dysarthria, pacing raised intelligibility for the more affected speakers ([PubMed 9755370](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9755370/)); metronome pacing reduces stuttering moments independently of the slower rate ([PMC4461240](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4461240/)).
- Stuttering: repetitions, prolongations and blocks ([ASHA](https://www.asha.org/practice-portal/clinical-topics/fluency-disorders/)). Cluttering: fast or irregular rate, collapsed syllables, many fillers, mid-sentence restarts ([ASHA Leader](https://leader.pubs.asha.org/do/10.1044/stuttering-versus-cluttering-whats-the-difference/full/)). Prolonged-speech programmes reduce stuttered syllables from about 5.7% to 1.0% immediately and 2.4% at a year ([Cocomazzo et al 2012](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1460-6984.2012.00150.x)). Easy onset and light contact are components of those programmes with no isolated trial ([ASHA](https://www.asha.org/practice-portal/clinical-topics/fluency-disorders/)). Delayed auditory feedback: a 2026 meta-analysis found no significant overall benefit ([Iimura et al](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1460-6984.70283)), so the app has none.

**What the app took.** Crispness is a spectral-tilt proxy (first-difference
energy over energy) as a percentage of the day's over-articulated warm-up
reading, so it compares him with himself on the same microphone; the CLEAR
goal is 85% of that reading, a number set here. Stammered starts are three or
more short bursts in a row; repeated words are read from the transcript. The
beat drill is the metronome. Nothing in the app is a clinical measure and it
says so.

## 6. Eye contact on camera

- Face to face, people hold eye contact about 61% of the time: 41% while talking, 75% while listening (Argyle & Cook 1976 via [Science of People](https://www.scienceofpeople.com/making-eye-contact/)). On camera, guides recommend about 80% on the lens with the rest for natural glances ([teleprompter.com](https://www.teleprompter.com/blog/how-to-maintain-eye-contact-in-videos)); no viewer-preference study for talking-head video was found.
- The one controlled study: viewers perceive eye contact best when the speaker looks about 2 degrees below the lens; perceived eye contact is lost about 4.5 degrees above and 5.5 below ([Journal of Vision 2025](https://jov.arvojournals.org/article.aspx?articleid=2810811), [PMC12439499](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12439499/)). At 60cm, 5 degrees is about 5cm.
- Techniques: prompter in front of the lens at eye level ([photographytalk](https://www.photographytalk.com/tips-on-using-a-teleprompter-a-guide-to-flawless-on-camera-presence/)); imagine one person ([backstage](https://www.backstage.com/magazine/article/how-to-confidently-speak-on-camera-78110/)); a sticker beside the lens ([teleprompter.com](https://www.teleprompter.com/blog/how-to-maintain-eye-contact-in-videos)).
- Estimating gaze in a browser: MediaPipe's face landmarker gives 478 points including the irises, and blendshapes for the eyes' look direction ([DEV](https://dev.to/metsander/mediapipe-face-mesh-all-478-landmark-points-5ec8)); Google states it "does not infer the location at which people are looking" ([Google Research](https://research.google/blog/mediapipe-iris-real-time-iris-tracking-depth-estimation/)). Head yaw under 20 degrees has been used as "toward the screen" ([arXiv 2504.06237](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.06237)). WebEyeTrack, MediaPipe plus a small model and nine calibration points, reaches about 2.3cm error ([arXiv 2508.19544](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.19544)), about the size of the window in which eye contact is perceived, so a browser can tell the lens zone from the screen or the room, not 2 degrees. Yoodli's eye-contact figure is reported unreliable in poor light and with glasses ([Final Round AI](https://www.finalroundai.com/blog/yoodli-review-pros-cons)).

**What the app took.** EYES step 1 asks for 60%, the face-to-face norm; step 3
for 80%, the camera norm, with glances under 2s. The lens zone is read during
the three-second count when the screen says to look at the lens, from the
eyes' look direction and the head's turn and tilt, and every look afterwards
is inside or outside that zone. The bands are a first guess and the brief says
how to tune them.

## 7. Duolingo, and daily practice in music apps

Duolingo's own material is marked (D).

- Learners with a 7-day streak are 2.4 times more likely to return the next day (D, [Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-streaks-keep-duolingo-learners-committed-to-their-language-goals/)). A/B tests (D via [Econsultancy](https://econsultancy.com/six-a-b-tests-used-by-duolingo-to-tap-into-habit-forming-behaviour/)): the streak always visible at the top, +3% daily actives; emphasised after every lesson, +3% day-14 retention.
- Streak freeze protects one missed day, two can be held ([Medium](https://medium.com/@salamprem49/duolingo-streak-system-detailed-breakdown-design-flow-886f591c953f)). The often-quoted "21% less churn" could not be traced to Duolingo and is not used here.
- The reminder that keeps winning is sent 23.5 hours after the previous day's first practice (D, [Sub Club](https://subclub.com/episode/how-to-time-reactivation-campaigns-for-maximum-impact-jackson-shuttleworth-duolingo)). A lesson takes about two minutes; daily goals are 5 to 20 minutes; the headline metric is learners spending 15 minutes a day "learning well" (D, [Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/time-spent-learning-well/)).
- The path is done in order; "Jump here?" skips ahead by passing a test ([Duolingo wiki](https://duolingo.fandom.com/wiki/Jump_here%3F)). The Practice Hub replays the learner's own mistakes and builds sessions on weak areas (D, [Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/guide-to-duolingo-practice-hub/)). Feedback on every answer is immediate.
- Yousician frames its streak as "daily practice is the biggest determinant of how fast you improve" ([Trophy](https://trophy.so/blog/streaks-feature-gamification-examples)); Simply Piano's streak needs one session a week and sits beside skill progress rather than alone ([Simply Piano](https://piano-help.hellosimply.com/en/articles/15496066-how-do-streaks-work-in-simply-piano)). Neither has a named daily warm-up.

**What the app took, and what the root brief forbids.** The path, the order,
the immediate numbers, the streak at the top, a freeze earned every five
practised days and spent silently, "Open this step now" as the jump, and
TODAY built from the skills furthest from target. No XP, no gems, no leagues,
no praise: the root brief's behaviour rules and its self-determination section
rule those out, and the "2.4 times" figure is about returning, not about
getting better.

## 8. Vocal warm-up drills

A three to five minute speaker warm-up, consistent across voice coaches ([Voice123](https://voice123.com/thebooth/vocal-warm-ups/), [Science of People](https://www.scienceofpeople.com/vocal-warm-ups/), [Vocalzone](https://www.vocalzone.com/the-record-blog/v-team-vocal-care/quick-daily-warm-ups/)): breath, a hiss on "sss" for 10, then 15, then 20 counts; lip trills of 10 to 15s; humming; sirens through the pitch range; jaw release; tongue twisters slow then fast, and over-enunciated reading. Order: breath and humming, then trills and sirens, then articulation.

Evidence: semi-occluded exercises (lip trill, humming, straw) as a warm-up reduced the activity of the muscles around the voice box in singers ([PubMed 34256979](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34256979/)); lip trill improved vocal measures after one session in a three-person study ([PMC11635114](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11635114/)); a systematic review of vocal warm-up found more sound pressure, longer maximum phonation time and cleaner harmonics ([SciELO](https://www.scielo.br/j/rcefac/a/47rW5K8NFbCgJSsYD4hNY4K/?lang=en)). Nothing tests tongue twisters or over-enunciation as a warm-up on speaking outcomes; they are articulation practice by tradition. Box breathing is general calming advice, not voice literature.

**What the app took.** Three measured steps: a hiss (longest sound, goal 15s,
the middle of the coaches' 10-15-20), a hum and siren (pitch spread, goal 10
semitones, set here), and three twisters over-articulated, which sets the day's
crispness reference. About three minutes.

## Confidence

High on the figures confirmed by two or more independent extracts: Bortfeld
5.97 per 100, Fox Tree and Shriberg about 6 per 100, the 250ms pause
threshold, the Journal of Vision 2 degrees, the prolonged-speech percentages,
