Start here

Five minutes, once. Then you never read this again.

This is a set of small apps for tracking training and daily habits. There is no account, no password, and no sign-up. You open a link and it works.

Everything you type is saved on your own phone. Nothing is uploaded and nobody else can see it, including the person who gave you this. That is the good news and the catch at the same time, and step 3 is about the catch.

1Put it on your home screen

Do this before anything else. Not because it looks nicer, but because phones delete websites they think you have stopped using. An iPhone clears a website's saved data after about a week of not visiting it. Adding it to your home screen tells the phone this is an app, and the data stays.

If you skip this step you may lose everything you have logged. It has nothing to do with these apps; every website on your phone works this way.

iPhone

  1. Open the link in Safari. It has to be Safari, not Chrome.
  2. Tap the share button at the bottom of the screen. It is the square with an arrow pointing up.
  3. Scroll down the list and tap Add to Home Screen.
  4. Tap Add.

Android

  1. Open the link in Chrome.
  2. Tap the three dots in the top right.
  3. Tap Add to Home screen, then Add.

From now on, open it from that home screen icon. Not from the link, not from a bookmark. The icon.

2Open TRAIN and answer one question

The first time you open TRAIN it asks whether you use kilograms or pounds. That is the only thing it cannot work out for itself. Answer it and you are set up. There is nothing else to configure.

You can change it later. It lives under the gear icon.

3Back it up every Sunday

This is the one habit that matters, and it takes about ten seconds.

  1. Go to the MAIN MENU, the home screen of the suite.
  2. Tap DATA in the top right.
  3. Tap Back up everything.
  4. A file downloads. Put it somewhere that is not your phone. Email it to yourself, or drop it in Google Drive or iCloud.

The DATA button changes to DATA ⚠ once it has been two weeks since your last backup. When you see the warning triangle, do the four steps above.

Why this matters. Your data is in one place: this phone. There is no server holding a copy. If the phone is lost, wiped, or you clear your browsing data, everything you logged is gone and there is no way to get it back.

A backup file is the only safety net there is. That is a deliberate trade: nothing about you leaves your phone unless you choose to send it.

What each app is for

AppWhat it does
TRAINYour training log. Exercises, sets, reps, weight, personal records. Built to be used one-handed while you are actually at the gym.
STATUSEverything else about a day: sleep, weight, steps, mood, food and spending. Also gives the day a grade from F to S.
BLOCKPlan a day as a set of blocks, and tick them off as you do them. It opens with one small example routine so you can see the shape. Delete it.
ARCA blank canvas for thinking. Notes and boxes you can join with lines. Best on a computer.
STYLEChanges how everything looks. Five themes. Purely cosmetic, and it cannot break anything.

TRAIN and STATUS are the two built for a phone. The other three are more comfortable on a computer, and they work on a phone if you need them.

Things that look broken but are not

  • It works with no signal. That is on purpose. Every tap saves immediately, on the phone, whether you have internet or not.
  • Nothing syncs between your phone and your laptop. They are two separate copies. Log on one of them, not both.
  • Screens start empty. There is nothing in there pretending to know how you should train or live. You fill it.
  • You can log any past day. Forgetting to log something at the time is normal. Go back and put it in.

What to send back

Anything at all, in whatever words come out. Useful things to say:

  • What you tried to do and could not work out how to do.
  • Anything that looked wrong, was slow, or made you stop and think.
  • A screenshot. Always worth more than a description.
  • What you stopped using after the first few days, and be honest about why.

Which phone you have and which app you were in is helpful, because a thing can be broken on one phone and fine on another.

If something goes badly wrong: your data is not inside the app, it is stored by your browser under the same web address. Closing the app, restarting the phone, or reopening the link will not lose anything. Clearing your browsing history will. If in doubt, make a backup first, then ask.
